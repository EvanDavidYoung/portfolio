I worked at Bloomberg for several years. The work was genuinely interesting — live audio infrastructure, video products, things that touched real users at scale. The people were smart. The pay was fine. I had no obvious reason to leave.

I quit anyway.

## The actual reason

I'd been wanting to learn Mandarin seriously for years. Not "do Duolingo in the morning" seriously — actually learn it, to the point of fluency. I'd started and stopped several times. What I kept running into was that language learning at the level I wanted required a kind of sustained, daily immersion that's very hard to manufacture in the margins of a full-time job.

The thing about software engineering jobs, even good ones, is that they consume a specific kind of mental bandwidth — the kind you also need to acquire a new language. By the time I was done with work, I didn't have the cognitive resources to study properly. I'd try for a few weeks, get somewhere, then let it slide when a project got intense. I'd done this cycle enough times that I knew I wasn't going to learn Mandarin while employed.

So I ran the math. I had savings. Taiwan is relatively affordable. I could take a year off, study seriously, and reenter the job market as someone who speaks Mandarin and has a few personal projects to show. The downside risk was a gap on my resume and a diminished savings account. The upside was something I'd wanted for years and kept deferring.

I gave notice.

## What I thought would be hard

I thought the career interruption would feel bad. It does, a little. I'm a person who likes building things and shipping them, and there's a version of that itch that doesn't get scratched by language study. I built some projects to stay sane — a transcription pipeline, a file organization tool, some small experiments. Nothing I'd put in front of a hiring committee, but enough to keep the hands busy.

I thought I'd second-guess the decision constantly. I haven't. Maybe that means it was the right one, or maybe it means I'm bad at second-guessing. I'm not sure how to tell.

## What I'm doing next

I'll go back to software engineering. I like the work. I'm more interested now in problems that touch language and communication and the gap between what people can say and what they can express — that feels meaningful in a way that rendering video didn't always.
